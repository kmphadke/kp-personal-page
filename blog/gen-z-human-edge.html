12 July 2025 · Talent & The Future of Work

Gen Z: The Human Edge Fuelling India's Digital Future

Recently, a senior executive at a leading Indian company confided, "We keep hearing about Gen Z's casual attitude being a problem. But I'm starting to wonder if we're missing something important." That question stuck with me — and it captures what I've seen as an academic and mentor working closely with young professionals.

Too often, employers focus on Gen Z's informal communication style and overlook the remarkable value they bring to the workplace. After years of observing talent trends, I've realised that our stereotypes about this generation often blind us to strengths that could transform our organisations.

The numbers tell a different story

Here's some context: by the end of 2025, Gen Z will make up 27% of India's workforce — an incredible 377 million strong. Their economic impact is huge: $250 billion in direct spending this year, projected to soar to $2 trillion by 2035. This isn't just a youth trend — it's the engine of India's economic future.

Gen Z's approach to learning is just as impressive: 85% are upskilling every week, and 91% are investing their own money in learning. They don't just show up — they show commitment.

Beyond IT: where Gen Z is making their mark

Yes, Gen Z is transforming tech — but their real impact is broader. In BFSI, their workforce share jumped from 12% to 23% in two years. They're digital natives: 68% use UPI daily, bringing real user experience to companies shaping India's digital future.

Healthcare is seeing a similar shift. While the world expects Gen Z to chase tech jobs, 75% of young professionals in India are opting for healthcare roles. They want more than a career — they want purpose, combining innovation with impact.

In e-commerce and digital media, their influence is unmistakable — media and entertainment is now Gen Z's top industry of choice. This isn't about glamour; it's about how digital-first thinking is reshaping business. With 70% of Gen Z influenced by social media in career choices, they intuitively understand modern markets.

The retention reality we need to address

What about loyalty? Despite the "job hopper" myth, 86% of BFSI employees say their organisations are great places to work, and 41% of Gen Z want to stay if the value proposition is right.

Disengagement isn't a loyalty problem — it's about unmet expectations. 34% of Gen Z feel disengaged, often because they aren't getting the growth opportunities they crave. They spend 73% more time learning AI skills than other generations, showing their hunger for growth.

What this means for Indian employers

It's time to shift the Gen Z conversation from concern to opportunity. Their directness is efficiency. Their digital fluency is essential to India's transformation. Their focus on values isn't naïve — it's what drives innovation.

For fintech companies, this means recognising that Gen Z employees understand digital banking not as a concept but as a lived experience. For healthcare organisations, it means leveraging their ability to see technology as a tool for human connection rather than replacement. For e-commerce companies, it means tapping into their instinctive understanding of how digital and physical experiences intersect.

The answer isn't to force Gen Z into old moulds, but to evolve our workplaces to tap into their unique strengths. The organisations that do this first will win the talent race — and fuel India's next phase of growth.

Let's stop asking if Gen Z is ready for us — and ask if we're ready to unlock their full potential.
