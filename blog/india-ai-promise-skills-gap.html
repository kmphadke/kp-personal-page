India's AI Promise: Strong on Potential, Still Closing the Skills Gap

Earlier this week, India's position in the QS World Future Skills Index 2027 was circulating across education feeds — 13th globally, among 89 countries. The optimism is understandable, and to a point it's earned.

But a second number in the same report tells a more complicated story — one I've been watching for years from my classroom.

India ranks 5th in the world. Just not for the reason you'd think.

On "Future of Work" — how well the country is positioned for an AI-driven economy — India scored 96.0 and ranked 5th globally. That's a real achievement: digital infrastructure, a large young workforce, and sustained government investment in skilling.

The problem shows up in "Skills Alignment" — how well the education system actually produces graduates employers can hire and deploy. There, India drops to 18th, with a score of 82.7, well below the top-ten average.

India produces talent at a vast scale, but employer satisfaction remains comparatively low relative to the pace at which the labour market is changing.

That 5th-versus-18th split is the real story for anyone in education — or in their first five years of working life.

The scale problem is also a quality problem

The IITs, IIMs and AIIMS compete at a world level — the QS data confirms it. But they're a small part of a very large system. Across thousands of colleges, graduates enter the workforce with credentials that don't fully map to what employers now ask for — especially as AI reshapes job descriptions faster than syllabi can follow. This isn't new. What's different is the pace at which the gap is widening.

What early-career professionals can do with this

The degree gets you to the interview. What tips the decision — consistently, in the roles I see my students pursue — is whether you can show working comfort with data tools, AI-assisted analysis, and clear communication.

These aren't reliably taught at scale yet. But they're expected on day one.

The graduates who stand out aren't waiting for the system to catch up. They're building that layer on top of their formal education — through projects, certifications, and deliberate practice. That self-direction is harder for any index to measure, but every hiring manager notices it.

The gap is real. So is the opportunity to close it on your own terms.

Source: QS World Future Skills Index 2027. Figures cited are drawn from the published index; readers are encouraged to consult the primary report for the full methodology.
