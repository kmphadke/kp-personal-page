Unlocking Innovation: The Rise of India's Tier 2 and 3 Cities

India's digital revolution is shifting fast. Coimbatore, Nagpur, Indore and Kochi — once off the tech radar — are now buzzing with innovation. IT hiring in these Tier 2 cities jumped 53% last year, nearly double the national average.

Innovation has found a new address

These cities are quickly becoming magnets for tech talent, outpacing the traditional IT hubs. Global names are setting up training centres and tapping into this fresh wave of energy, as each city finds its own place in the tech ecosystem.

Robotics and data science roles are booming beyond the metros. Kochi, Ahmedabad and Coimbatore now account for nearly 70% of Tier 2 hiring in these fields — unlocking fresh opportunities for professionals. With better digital infrastructure and remote work, demand for cybersecurity, cloud, and software skills is soaring. Companies are searching far and wide for new talent.

Fintech's new frontier

Fintech is taking off, too — card spending in places like Howrah and Tirupur is up 175% since 2019, and online spending in small cities has surged. Bhubaneswar is also on the tech map, thanks to supportive government initiatives. Banking tech is booming, and roles in investment banking and business analysis are hotter than ever.

Nearly half of fintech startups now come from smaller cities — a huge leap from a decade ago. Stories like Finvasia in Chandigarh prove you can build world-class products from anywhere. Even blockchain technology is finding roots in these places, opening doors for new ideas. Major tech companies are taking notice: TCS has expanded well beyond the metros, and IBM is following suit, opening centres to tap into local talent and offer training opportunities.

Solving real, local problems

Government support is making a real difference — funding, infrastructure, and local initiatives are helping startups and small businesses thrive. Data centres in cities like Patna and Lucknow are strengthening the foundation for even more growth. Teams in these cities are stable and productive, thanks to lower attrition and a better cost of living.

But it's more than that — India's brightest are solving real local problems. Gramophone is helping Indore's farmers, while DealShare makes household goods affordable for small towns. These solutions are building loyalty and sustainable growth.

The road to 2035

By 2035, more than half of India's startups are expected to come from these cities, creating millions of jobs. Digital payments could triple, with much of this momentum coming from outside the metros. These cities aren't just catching up — they're leading the way. Their success proves innovation truly begins at home.

For IT and fintech professionals, opportunity is everywhere. Every city can be a tech hub.

If you're building, hiring, or simply curious, now's the time to look beyond the metros.
